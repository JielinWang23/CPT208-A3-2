## 👥 User Persona

| User Group | Core Pain Points | Core Needs |
|------------|-------------------|------------|
| **Child** | Museum exhibits are "look-only" and distant from daily life, leading to a boring experience. | Desire hands-on interaction and playful engagement, avoiding passive, didactic explanations. |
| **Young Adult** | Outdated exhibition design, less efficient information access than web searches, and unengaging display formats. | Seek gamified challenges or modern interactive formats (e.g., comment-style interactions) to spark curiosity. |



## 🎬 Museum Interest Survey Results

| Survey Item | Data Result | Key Insight |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Interested in visiting a museum** | 28% | A minority of young people hold a positive attitude |
| **Not interested in visiting a museum** | **72%** | Over two-thirds of students lack interest |
| **Reason for not being interested** | 92% find museums "too boring" | Lack of engagement is the core issue |
| **Suggested improvement** | Most prefer: more interactivity / check-in activities | Interactive experiences and gamification could significantly boost interest |

### Conclusion
Over 70% of young people are not interested in museums, with the vast majority finding the experience too dull. Introducing **interactive elements** (e.g., AR guides, touchscreens) or **check-in activities** (e.g., stamp collection, photo spots, challenges) would effectively increase their willingness to visit.




## 🎬 Museum Visitor Interviews: Key Insights

To better understand why young people find museums uninteresting and what changes would encourage them to visit, we conducted five interviews with individuals aged 12 to 26. The findings are summarized below.

### Interview Summaries

| ID | Age / Role | Museum Experience | Suggested Improvement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A** | 26 / Internet Operations | Boring. Weekend is for relaxing, but the museum still feels like "learning." Walked through quickly and forgot everything. | **AR-powered "effort-saving" experience.** Point phone at an artifact to see a 30-second highlight video explaining what it is, why it matters, and how it relates to me. Short and engaging, like scrolling through TikTok. |
| **B** | 22 / Senior University Student | The worst part is just "looking." Glass cases + labels = too much information. Feels like passive learning, not active exploration. Tired legs, empty mind. | **Gamification with team challenges.** Scan codes to form teams, answer quiz questions, and compete on a leaderboard. Winners get shout-outs on a big screen with fun messages. Would bring friends to join. |
| **C** | 20 / Sophomore University Student | Quite boring. Dim lighting, stuffy atmosphere, have to walk quietly. Artifacts feel completely unrelated to daily life. Prefers shopping or gaming at home. | **Immersive AR "time travel."** Stand in front of an artifact and use AR to "teleport" to that era — see the environment, hear the sounds. Feels like being there, not just looking through glass. Cool enough to share a short video on social media. |
| **D** | 12 / Middle School Student (Grade 7) | Boring. Can't understand the displays. Can't touch or interact with anything — "like walking through a supermarket, but without the snacks." | **Level-based mini-games.** After viewing each exhibit, answer a quiz question on your phone to unlock the next "level." Makes the visit feel like completing a game, creating curiosity for what comes next. |
| **E** | 14 / Middle School Student (Grade 8) | Too much walking and reading. Eyes get tired. Pots and jars feel distant and untouchable — loses interest quickly and wants to check their phone. | **Living artifacts.** Scan with a phone to make the artifact "come alive" — animated, moving, and talking like a cartoon. "That would be a hundred times more fun!" |

### Key Takeaways

| Theme | Summary |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Pain Point** | Young visitors find museums **passive, text-heavy, and disconnected** from their daily lives. |
| **Desired Experience** | **Short, interactive, and immersive** — like social media feeds or video games. |
| **Top Requests** | AR quick-view videos, team-based quizzes, time-travel immersion, level-based games, and animated artifacts. |

### Conclusion

Across all age groups (12–26), young people consistently want museums to shift from **passive observation** to **active participation**. Short-form AR content, gamification, and immersive storytelling were the most frequently requested improvements. Implementing these features could significantly increase young visitors' willingness to attend and engage with museum exhibits.

# 🚀 Version 2.0

## Key Updates

### 1. Gamification & UX Optimization

The initial UI design prioritized the integration of AI with exhibit introductions. While AR gameplay was included in the original concept, we found that designing unique mini-games for each individual exhibit resulted in excessively high development costs and long iteration cycles.

To address this, we optimized the gamification strategy in v2.0.0:

- **Homepage Achievement Showcase**  
  Moved the check-in achievement system to the homepage for prominent display, significantly boosting user engagement and interactive fun without the overhead of per-exhibit game development.

- **AR Real-world Fun Map**  
  Added an AR-powered interactive map that overlays the museum's physical space, helping visitors easily locate and navigate to check-in exhibits, improving the overall on-site experience.

### 2. Cost & Efficiency Improvement

- **Removed high-cost per-exhibit game design**  
  Eliminated the resource-intensive plan of building custom games for each exhibit, reducing development workload while retaining core gamification value.

- **Unified achievement system**  
  Implemented a centralized, scalable check-in and reward system that is easy to maintain and iterate for future updates.

## UnityAR

### unityAR development
For more details, please refer to the GitHub page.https://github.com/kisekiqiji/cpt208A3-2UnityAr
### Vibe Coding
The root object XR Origin (AR Rig) serves as the core tracking system, containing a Camera Offset transform node that adjusts the camera's physical position relative to the AR tracking origin. Within the offset, the Main Camera handles AR visual rendering and pose tracking, while the Screen Space Ray Interactor enables touch-based ray interaction for placing virtual objects in the real world. The Object Spawner component manages the instantiation and placement of AR content, triggered by user input. The AR Session object controls the AR lifecycle, including session initialization, tracking state management, and plane detection. Additional components such as Directional Light and UI provide basic scene lighting and user interface support, ensuring stable visual feedback and interactive experience.
