Motivation and Research

🔍 Research Background & Design Motivation

With the deep integration of digital technology and museum scenarios, AR navigation and AI interpretation have become effective ways to attract young visitors. Nevertheless, most existing museum solutions face common limitations, including homogenized content, weak interactivity, and poor lightweight experience. Traditional AR game development requires high costs and causes heavy performance consumption, while mainstream museum applications rely on single textual or audio guidance, lacking attractive interactive mechanisms.

This project focuses on the core pain point that modern young users generally regard museum visits as boring and passive. We design a lightweight WebAR museum system based on Unity and WebGL. By adopting AR scene recognition, intelligent AI explanation, and gamified check-in mechanics, the project delivers an immersive and low-threshold visiting experience without downloading additional applications, helping young users better understand cultural heritage and increasing long-term museum engagement.

Design Concept & Visual Gallery

In the early design stage, the team tried diverse styles including traditional Chinese aesthetics and dark-mode interfaces. After comparison, a concise and modern visual style was finally selected to adapt to dim museum lighting and AR superposition effects. To reduce development pressure and improve operational efficiency, the project abandoned the high-cost solution of designing independent mini-games for each exhibit. Instead, a unified check-in and achievement system was constructed. Combined with AR real-world positioning and interactive maps, it balances development cost, user experience and long-term iteration. The overall design focuses on simplicity, accessibility and social sharing, making museum visits more interesting, relaxing and educational for young audiences.

🏛️ System Architecture Overview

The system adopts a three‑layer architecture: Presentation Layer (WebGL frontend with AR camera & UI), Logic Layer (check‑in engine, achievement management, API gateway for AI), and Data Layer (exhibit database, user progress). All core interaction—AR recognition, badge unlocking, navigation overlays—is processed locally with minimal latency, while AI calls are asynchronous.

🛠️ Key Technical Challenges & Solutions

  • Performance on WebGL: Optimized 3D models, reduced draw calls, and implemented texture compression to sustain 30+ FPS on mid‑range devices.
  • AR Tracking Stability: Enhanced target image feature points and added gyroscope fallback for smoother tracking in low light.
  • AI Response Delay: Used streaming TTS and local cache of common exhibit descriptions to improve real‑time feedback.
  • Cross‑browser Compatibility: Adapted camera API and WebRTC policies for Safari and Chrome on iOS/Android.

💡 Reflection & Future Improvement

According to the standardized evaluation (SUS average 82.5, MES high scores in “meaningful interaction”, UEQ-S positive), users acknowledged the WebAR lightweight design and gamified experience. However, some interviewees desired deeper mini‑game integration and more social sharing features. In future iterations, we plan to:

  • Add lightweight AR mini‑games for key exhibits (e.g., relic restoration puzzles).
  • Introduce a friend ranking system for badges to enhance social drive.
  • Further optimize AI response personalization based on visitor’s age group.
  • Extend support to more museums via open recognition target library.

✅ Overall Conclusion: The project successfully validated that a browser‑based AR check‑in + AI interpretation system can effectively increase young visitors’ engagement and reduce perceived boredom, providing a scalable solution for future digital museum transformation.
